# Areas: Poly3D against the ribbon-strip fill

`BenchAreas.qml`, recorded 2026-08-04. Raw per-sample rows in
`areas-2026-08-04.csv`.

Before `Poly3D` existed (#183), an area was faked by covering it with a fan of
flat `LineBatch3D` ribbons plus a hand-tuned per-strip depth ramp to stop the
coplanar strips z-fighting. #183's checklist asserts a ground polygon "should be
measurably cheaper than the strip-fill it replaces". **It is not, at scale.**

## Machine / method

- Apple M-series, macOS 26.5.2, Qt 6.11.1, Metal.
- Run in **claydojo**, not `clayrender`: `clayrender` drives its own fixed frame
  cadence and pins `frame_ms` near 52 ms for every mode and count, measuring the
  renderer rather than the scene. Only `build_ms` is meaningful there.
- Same seed, same polygons, same camera for both modes. Steps alternate
  strips/poly at each count so machine drift hits both.
- 20 strips per area, all areas in **one** `LineBatch3D` batch — the fairest
  version of the workaround. One `LineBatch3D` per area would be worse.
- 2 s warmup, 8 s per step, first third of samples dropped. Camera rotates
  slowly so the scene never idles; vsync caps at ~65 fps.

## What it says

- **Render cost is not the differentiator below ~1000 areas.** Both hold vsync.
  Anything in the range a lab or a map actually uses is free either way.
- **Past that, the strip fill wins.** At 2000 areas `Poly3D` drops to 54.8 fps
  while the strips hold 64.8. The reason is draw calls: 2000 `Poly3D`s are 2000
  `Model`s and 2000 draw calls, against one instanced batch. This is the
  concrete argument for `PolyBatch3D` (#185), and the number to beat.
- **`Poly3D` builds about 2.4x faster** — 52 ms against 126 ms at 2000 areas —
  because the strip fill has to scanline every ring into 40,000 ribbon segments
  and ramp their depths.

So the honest case for `Poly3D` over the workaround is *correctness and
authoring*, not throughput: it expresses holes, needs no per-strip depth ramp,
cannot band, and is one object rather than N ribbons. Its scale ceiling is real
and is documented as tens of areas, not thousands.
